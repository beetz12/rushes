## What it does

BetterShot is a free menu-bar app for macOS. It started as a CleanShot-style screenshot tool and grew into a recorder and video editor. On the screenshot side it takes region, window, full-screen and scrolling captures. It can freeze the screen while you select an area. It reads text from the screen with OCR and picks screen colours. Captures land in a floating preview deck where you copy, save, pin, edit, drag or share them. The image editor offers arrows, shapes, text, numbered markers, highlight, blur and pixelate, plus wallpaper or gradient framing. A smart-redaction pass finds secrets with on-device OCR.

On the recording side it captures a display, a window or a dragged area with ScreenCaptureKit. It writes the screen, and optionally system audio and the microphone, into one fragmented QuickTime master. The webcam goes into a separate camera movie, and pointer and shortcut input go into a JSON sidecar. A compact floating bar handles start, pause, restart and discard. A teleprompter hangs from the MacBook notch and scrolls as you speak. After you stop, the recording opens in a "Studio" editor. There you cut clips and change their speed, add zoom cues, masks, crop, captions and 3D camera shots, restyle the cursor, and arrange screen and camera in six layouts. The editor then renders an MP4 or MOV.

Sharing is optional and bring-your-own-storage. The app uploads the rendered file, a poster frame and a small JSON manifest to the user's own Cloudflare R2 bucket over hand-signed S3 requests. It then copies a link to a viewer page on the project's website, and that page reads the manifest straight from the user's bucket. Outside sharing, the README says the only network call is the update check against GitHub releases.

## Feature inventory

- **capture-screen (4):** ScreenCaptureKit display, window or area streams at up to 60 fps in BGRA. The app's own windows are left out of the capture, and the OS cursor is left out of the pixels. The writer produces HEVC in a .mov with 2-second movie fragments. The bitrate is the larger of 20 Mbps or width times height times 4. Screenshots add a frozen-frame selector, scrolling capture (GPL-derived, see Weaknesses), OCR and a colour picker.
- **capture-camera (4, shared core):** An AVCaptureSession at a 720p preset writes HEVC to its own `camera.mov` inside the session folder. A 160-point circular preview panel floats on screen and is excluded from capture. The camera's start offset against the screen movie is computed from the shared host clock.
- **capture-audio (3):** System audio comes from ScreenCaptureKit with the app's own audio excluded. The microphone also comes through ScreenCaptureKit, which only works on macOS 15 or later. Each source goes to its own AAC track at 48 kHz in the screen master: stereo at 192 kbps and mono at 96 kbps. Missing or denied devices are turned off before capture starts, with a warning. The editor can import a replacement soundtrack and export audio only as M4A or WAV.
- **recording-controls (4):** One shared floating bar. Its picker strip holds Display, Window and Area, then Camera, Mic, Audio and Script toggles. There are separate countdown delays for screenshots and recordings, and global shortcuts for stop, pause, restart and discard. Restart and discard need a native confirmation. The app never starts a recording after a cancel, and quitting finishes and saves an active recording.
- **camera-overlay (4):** Six layouts: Bubble, Overlap, Side-by-Side, Presenter, Camera Only and Screen Only. Six camera aspect ratios are independent of the canvas, and size, roundness, position and left/right mirroring can all be changed. One deterministic layout function serves both preview and export.
- **annotation (4 for stills):** Rich image annotation that stays editable through crop, rotate and flip. Video only gets blur or pixelate masks and burned-in captions. You cannot draw live during a recording.
- **compositing-render (4):** Core Image on Metal composites backgrounds, padding, rounded corners, shadows, cursor restyling, click ripples, keystroke captions, subtitles and masks. An AGPL-derived 3D-shot renderer sits on top.
- **editing (4):** A non-destructive clip list over the untouched source, with per-clip speed from 0.25x to 8x. Zoom cues can be automatic or manual. Also crop, masks, transcript-driven cuts, undo and redo, and a debounced draft autosave kept apart from the explicit save.
- **transcription-captions (3):** On-device SpeechAnalyzer, macOS 26 only, with word timings. Cues hold at most 42 characters and last at most 4 seconds. Captions can show a karaoke-style word highlight and are burned into the export. There is no SRT or VTT export.
- **ai-features (3):** Everything runs on the device, with no LLM: OCR, smart redaction, transcription, teleprompter voice tracking, filler and silence cut suggestions, click-driven auto zoom, and an automatic 3D scene sequence.
- **export-formats (3):** MP4 (the default) or MOV, in H.264 or HEVC, at 30 or 60 fps. Resolution can stay original or drop to 1080p, 720p or 480p, under three quality levels. Reframe presets cover landscape, vertical, square and 4:5, each with fill or fit. Audio-only export is M4A or WAV, and stills are PNG or JPEG. There is no GIF or WebM.
- **project-format (4):** Each recording is a `.bettershotrec` folder. It holds the screen master, the camera movie, a capture manifest, the input sidecar, a saved edit document, a draft edit document, project metadata, a render stamp and a poster.
- **sharing-hosting (4):** The user brings their own R2 bucket. Requests are signed with SigV4 in about 100 lines and need no SDK. Credentials live in the Keychain, and EU or FedRAMP jurisdiction endpoints are supported. The user can choose a viewer link or a direct file link, and can delete a share.
- **viewer-web (3):** A Next.js route on the project's own domain reads the manifest from the user's bucket on the server. It renders Open Graph and Twitter player cards, and a download route proxies the file. There are no comments, view counts, passwords or expiry.
- **teleprompter-scripts (4, shared core):** The script overlay grows out of the notch, or shows as a pill on displays without one. It advances by matching live on-device transcription against the script.
- **platform-packaging (3):** XcodeGen and Make, signed and notarised DMGs for both architectures, and a Homebrew cask. A custom GitHub-release updater checks the Developer ID team before it installs. The app runs as a menu-bar agent with the hardened runtime and no sandbox.
- **performance-reliability (4):** Fragmented masters survive crashes, and a recovery scan runs at launch. A stream error keeps the footage already captured. Writer failures are detected on the write path. Synchronous backpressure stops frame build-up, and an activity assertion stops App Nap and idle sleep. The export pipeline has published benchmarks.
- **extensibility-automation (2):** A `bettershot://` URL scheme triggers captures. The record route only opens the picker. There is no stop route, no CLI and no JSON output. The repo ships agent rule files and vendored skill packs.
- **privacy-security (4):** Local-first, and plain typing is never recorded. Secrets go to the Keychain, and the app's own windows stay out of captures. Upload needs an explicit consent toggle, and the viewer has an SSRF guard. Shared links are public to anyone who has them.

## Architecture

The app is a single process with a menu-bar agent. Singletons coordinate it, and each one owns a surface: the capture orchestrator for screenshots, the screen recording manager for video, the camera manager, the teleprompter controller, the bar presenter, the studio project registry, the R2 uploader and credential store, and the history store.

**Recording data flow.** A recording entry point first runs an optional countdown. The manager then resolves its inputs before it creates anything. It checks the OS version for microphone support, re-reads device catalogues, and requests microphone and camera permission. Inputs that fail are dropped with a warning, so an optional input cannot break the screen recording. Next it creates a session folder in Application Support, not in a temp directory, so a crash does not hand the footage to the temp purger. It names the deliverable once at this point, then starts the camera, then the pointer and shortcut recorders, and only then the ScreenCaptureKit stream. That order keeps setup UI and permission prompts out of the first frames. Video and audio callbacks run on dedicated queues and push into one writer through a synchronous hop. If the encoder falls behind, the stream's small queue drops frames instead of holding full-size surfaces in memory. The writer zeroes timestamps against the first video frame. It discards audio that arrives before that frame, subtracts paused time, and checks the writer's health on every append. When the recording stops, the manager writes the capture manifest with the camera lead-in and the input sidecar. Sidecar times are mapped from the host clock onto the movie's own timeline.

**Studio.** The editor model loads the effective edit document, which is the draft if one exists, otherwise the saved one. Every view of the project, including preview, export and upload, goes through that one document. The deterministic layout and viewport maths are shared by the live preview and the offline exporter, so what you see is what gets exported. The exporter decodes the screen and camera movies and walks a fixed output clock at 30 or 60 fps. It composites each tick in Core Image straight into NV12 buffers backed by IOSurface, keeps three frames in flight, and encodes with the hardware encoder. Audio is mixed and passed through unchanged unless a replacement track was imported. The finished render is moved into the package together with a render stamp, and Save, Copy and Share all reuse it while the stamp still matches the edits.

**Sharing.** The share step compresses stills locally, keeping whichever of PNG or JPEG comes out smaller. It then uploads three objects under a per-share prefix: the media file, a poster JPEG and a JSON manifest. The share id is a URL-safe base64 form of a UUID. The link points at the project's viewer with the bucket origin packed into a query parameter, so the website stores nothing. Deleting a share reads the manifest back to find the media name, then deletes all three objects.

**Why it is built this way.** Code comments state four reasons. A recording that has started must never be lost silently. The source media must never be modified, so every edit can be redone. Preview and export must share their maths. Upload is opt-in, and it is the user's own storage.

## Best ideas

1. **Own-bucket share links with a stateless hosted viewer** (sharing-hosting; copy-with-notice; must). The app needs only an S3-style bucket. It uploads the media, a poster and a small manifest under one prefix, and builds a viewer link that carries the bucket origin as a base64url query value. The viewer fetches the manifest on the server and refuses bad origins: anything that is not https, private or link-local address ranges, oversized manifests and slow responses. This fits David's "storage he controls" rule without running any server of his own. The signing code (`Sources/Sharing/R2Uploader.swift:10-117`) has no dependencies and has tests (`Tests/R2SigV4Check.swift`). The build team could host the viewer route themselves instead of using bettershot.site.
2. **Six camera layout presets from one pure layout function** (camera-overlay; copy-with-notice; must). Bubble, Overlap, Side-by-Side (camera takes half the width), Presenter (camera takes about a third and the screen card is padded), Camera Only and Screen Only. The camera aspect ratio is independent of the canvas ratio. The layout falls back to screen only when camera footage is missing, and a mirror switch puts the camera on the left. Because the camera is a separate file, the user can pick a layout after recording. That is exactly how David's editor already works. See `Sources/BetterShot/RecordingStudioStyle.swift:432-576`.
3. **Render stamp to prove a cached export is fresh** (performance-reliability; copy-with-notice; should). The package stores the exact edit document that produced the flattened file. Share and Export reuse the file only when the current document matches the stamp. When only the container changed, the file is remuxed instead of re-rendered. This makes the second share instant and stops anyone uploading stale edits. See `Sources/BetterShot/RecordingSession.swift:255-282`.
4. **Export on a fixed output clock with a bounded GPU pipeline** (export-formats; copy-with-notice; should). Each output tick re-renders the newest source frame, so zoom animations keep moving through stretches where ScreenCaptureKit sent no new frames. Three frames stay in flight, buffers are NV12 IOSurfaces, colour is tagged as sRGB or Rec. 709, and MP4 gets the fast-start index for streaming. The repo's own benchmark shows a 3 to 4 times speedup on heavy effects. See `Sources/BetterShot/RecordingStudioExporter.swift:377-497` and `docs/export-performance.md`.
5. **A teleprompter that follows your voice** (teleprompter-scripts; copy-with-notice, but this code is nearly identical to Screendrop's CC0 copy, so take it from there; should). Microphone buffers are teed off the recording stream, so there is no second capture session. They feed on-device streaming recognition. A greedy matcher with an eight-word lookahead is re-run over the whole spoken history on every update, and the displayed position can only move forward. The overlay is excluded from capture, and the speech model downloads when the teleprompter is switched on, not when Record is pressed. See `Sources/BetterShot/TeleprompterScript.swift:36-79`.
6. **Click-driven auto zoom on spring-smoothed viewports** (editing; clean-room, because the file is tagged AGPL; should). Each mouse-down becomes an editable zoom cue with a short lead-in and a longer hold. Cues that sit close together merge into one continuous zoom. Magnification and centre then follow separate damped springs, integrated at a fixed rate along the edited timeline. Cuts therefore never interrupt a camera move, and the frame never leaves the source image. Re-implement this from the description only. See `Sources/BetterShot/RecordingViewportTimeline.swift:1-30` for the licence header.
7. **Transcript-planned cuts** (editing; copy-with-notice, shared with Screendrop; should). Word timings become cut ranges for three things: selected words, a deliberately short list of filler sounds, and pauses longer than about 1.1 seconds. Each cut keeps some breathing room around it, and very short cuts are skipped. The cuts remove source ranges from the clip list and never touch the media. See `Sources/BetterShot/RecordingTranscriptEditing.swift:13-40`.
8. **Silence detection that adapts to the speaker's level** (editing; copy-with-notice; could). The audio is measured in 50 ms windows. The speech level is taken as the 95th percentile, silence is anything 25 dB below that, and nothing is cut if the loudest window stays below -45 dB. This needs no speech model, so it works on macOS 14. It is unwired at this SHA: only a test calls it. See `Sources/Services/SilenceDetector.swift`.
9. **Private staging and names chosen once** (project-format; copy-with-notice; could). Every capture first lands in a private folder. Only an explicit save writes a deliverable. The file name is rendered once from a template with date, time, counter, hex and uuid tokens. Later saves overwrite the same exported file, found through the path stored in the project, instead of scattering copies. See `Sources/BetterShot/ScreenshotFileNaming.swift` and `Sources/BetterShot/RecordingDeliverable.swift`.
10. **On-device smart redaction rules** (privacy-security; copy-with-notice; could). OCR finds text, then patterns plus validators flag emails, URLs, IPv4 addresses, phone numbers, card numbers (checked with Luhn), JWTs, well-known API-key prefixes, long opaque tokens, and "password: value" pairs. These could seed an automatic blur-before-share check for videos. See `Sources/BetterShot/SmartRedactionRecognizer.swift:100-117`.
11. **Strict URL-scheme automation** (extensibility-automation; copy-with-notice; could). Routes are a closed enum. The parser rejects user, password, port, query and fragment parts. Actions arriving before launch completes are queued, and a request to record during an active recording is ignored. This is a safe base for an agent-facing control surface, although it lacks a stop command and any way to read results back. See `Sources/App/CaptureURLAction.swift`.
12. **Do not wait for Spotlight after screencapture** (capture-screen; copy-with-notice; could). The runner returns as soon as the PNG on disk is complete, because the system tool can sit for up to 10 seconds waiting on Spotlight. The exit status still decides whether the capture was cancelled. See `Sources/Capture/ScreencaptureRunner.swift`.
13. **Updater that checks the signing team** (platform-packaging; copy-with-notice; could). Before it installs a downloaded release, the updater validates the app bundle against a code requirement that pins the developer's team identifier. See `Sources/Services/AppUpdater.swift:240-262`.

## Weaknesses and gaps

- **Licence contamination risk for the build team.** sources.json classes this repo as permissive, but these files declare AGPL-3.0-only as adaptations of Cap: `Sources/BetterShot/Recording3DShot.swift`, `Recording3DInspector.swift`, `Recording3DBlurRenderer.swift` and `RecordingViewportTimeline.swift`, the last being the zoom engine. These scroll-capture files say they are adapted from MacShot under GPLv3: `Sources/Capture/ScrollCaptureController.swift`, `ScrollFrameAnalyzer.swift` and `ScrollCapturePreviewPanel.swift`. `RecordingTimelineInteraction.swift` and parts of `RecordingStudioWindow.swift` describe their maths as Cap's own transforms but carry no tag. `Tests/Fixtures/Cap3DReference.json` was generated by compiling Cap. My recommendation: build sessions should not open these files, and the plan stage should consider adding them to a copyleft exclusion list. As distributed, the app bundle is arguably AGPL as a whole.
- **Shared lineage.** Screendrop's `RecordingViewportTimeline.swift` is about 83 percent line-similar to the AGPL-tagged copy here, but it has no AGPL header. I did not establish which came first. The plan stage should treat zoom code from either repo with care.
- **No live drawing during recording, and no live camera composite.** The bubble is only a preview window, and viewers see the camera only after a Studio render. A Loom-style "stop, then the link is ready" flow therefore always costs a render.
- **No microphone capture on macOS 14.** The only narration path goes through ScreenCaptureKit, so on macOS 14 recordings have no voice. Transcription, captions and teleprompter tracking need macOS 26.
- **Weak automation.** The record route only opens the picker. There is no stop, no status, no JSON and no CLI.
- **Viewer is thin and centrally hosted.** Links point at bettershot.site by default. Anyone with a link can see the file: there is no expiry, password or view tracking.
- **Possible audio retiming issue (unverified).** The screen writer retimes audio with a single timing entry whose duration is the whole buffer's duration. The voom repo documents this exact pattern as a bug that stretches per-sample durations. I did not test whether AVAssetWriter actually misbehaves here.
- **Dead code.** The adaptive silence detector is only called by a test. Some Info.plist usage text mentions "voice screenshots", a feature I could not find.
- **Instructions inside the repo, which I did not follow.** `AGENTS.md` tells agents to push straight to main. The vendored skill READMEs tell readers to run an npx installer. I treated both as data only.
- **The skill packs are generic.** `.agents/skills` and `.claude/skills` are identical folders. They hold third-party design and Swift guidance pinned by hash in `skills-lock.json` (Apple-style motion, anti-"AI slop" design, landing-page design, macOS development, Swift style, a logo generator). Nothing in them is specific to recording. The useful agent artefact is `AGENTS.md` itself, a dense list of capture and persistence invariants and honest-verification rules.
- **Verification limits of this note.** I read code and docs only; I built and ran nothing. The quality scores and performance numbers come from source reading and the repo's own benchmark document.
